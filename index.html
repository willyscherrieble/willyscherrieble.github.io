About

I'm an economist in the financial stability department at Deutsche Bundesbank, specializing in systemic risk, market microstructure, and the use of big data for policy analysis and automated risk monitoring. I also bring prior experience in company leadership and data science consulting. My research uses large-scale transaction data to study the structure of financial markets and the behavior of investors. I earned my PhD in Economics from Universitat Pompeu Fabra (Barcelona School of Economics) in 2020 and a Bachelor in Business Administration from EBS University in 2014.

Working Papers

Anatomy of a Safe Asset: Bund Market Structure and Reform

Mark Kerssenfischer (Deutsche Bundesbank), Konrad Lucke (Goethe University), and Willy Scherrieble (Deutsche Bundesbank) — November 2026 — Deutsche Bundesbank Discussion Paper No. 36/2026

Read the paper at the Bundesbank →

This paper constructs a non-anonymous transaction-level dataset for German Bunds, the euro area's benchmark safe asset, from MiFID I and MiFID II transaction reports since 2008. We show that a concentrated dealer core intermediates this safe asset through persistent dealer–client relationships. Trading is segmented across investor sectors, with order flow fragmented across bilateral OTC and electronic venues. Identified stress flows show foreign official accounts supplying Bunds whether prices fall in a dash for cash or rise in a flight to quality. Hedge funds absorb Bunds in both episodes, while primary dealers absorb Covid selling but distribute Bunds after fiscal supply news. Hedge funds and official sector accounts carry the most permanent price-relevant flow yet pay middling effective half-spreads, while retail households pay by far the highest. Execution costs do not increase monotonically with the price content of flow. We evaluate two reforms targeting this structure: mandatory central clearing would cut dealers' net settlement obligations in Bunds by 35%, less than comparable estimates for U.S. Treasuries and U.K. Gilts. In the benchmark structural calibration, platform centralization lowers welfare because matching gains from broader dealer access do not offset lost relationship surplus under imperfect dealer competition.

Who Clears the Market When Pro-Cyclical Investors Trade?

Johannes Beutel (Deutsche Bundesbank) and Willy Scherrieble (Deutsche Bundesbank) — February 2026

Read the paper on SSRN →

Limits of arbitrage can explain why hedge funds and other investors do not take large counter-cyclical positions in the aggregate equity market, but they leave open the question of who buys when markets crash and who sells during booms. We address this long-standing puzzle using transaction-level data on the universe of trades across major European equity and interest rate cash and derivatives markets. We classify investors into sectors and quantify, at horizons from 15 minutes to one month, which sectors absorb the net flows of the most pro-cyclical sector (asset managers). We find dealer banks are the dominant liquidity providers across markets. At daily frequency, they absorb nearly the entire net flow of asset managers, while principal trading firms and hedge funds absorb only about 10% and 5%, respectively. Long-term investors trade pro-cyclically, amplifying rather than offsetting flows. Our results provide direct evidence for the conjecture in the intermediary asset pricing literature that dealer banks are key liquidity providers across different asset classes.
