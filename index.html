About

I'm an economist in the financial stability department at Deutsche Bundesbank, with deep expertise in systemic risk, market microstructure, and the application of advanced analytics to financial stability monitoring. My research uses large-scale transaction-level data to study the structure of financial markets and the behavior of investors. I earned my PhD in Economics from Universitat Pompeu Fabra (Barcelona School of Economics) in 2020 and a Bachelor in Business Administration from EBS University in 2014.

Working Papers

Anatomy of a Safe Asset: Bund Market Structure and Reform

Mark Kerssenfischer (Deutsche Bundesbank), Konrad Lucke (Goethe University), and Willy Scherrieble (Deutsche Bundesbank) — June 2026

This paper constructs a non-anonymous transaction-level dataset for German Bunds, the euro area's benchmark safe asset, from regulatory reports since 2008. We show how this safe asset actually trades: activity is organized around a concentrated dealer core, segmented across investor sectors, and fragmented across venues. In stress, identified flows reveal who supplies the safe asset and who absorbs it: foreign official accounts sell Bunds whether prices fall in a dash for cash or rise in a flight to quality, while hedge funds and the dealer core take the other side. Hedge funds and official sector accounts carry the most permanent price-relevant flow, yet pay middling effective half-spreads, while retail households pay by far the highest effective half-spreads: execution costs do not increase monotonically with the price content of flow. We evaluate two reforms that target this structure: mandatory central clearing would cut dealers' settlement obligations in Bunds even more than comparable estimates for U.S. Treasuries and U.K. Gilts; in the benchmark structural calibration, platform centralization lowers welfare under estimated relationship surplus and imperfect dealer competition, while alternative assumptions about dealer competition, relationship value, or demand elasticity can reverse the sign.

Presentations: Bundesbank Term Structure Workshop (2025); 32nd Annual Meeting of the German Finance Association (DGF), Düsseldorf (September 2026)

Who Clears the Market When Pro-Cyclical Investors Trade?

Johannes Beutel (Deutsche Bundesbank), Willy Scherrieble (Deutsche Bundesbank), and Maik Schmeling (Goethe University) — February 2026

Limits of arbitrage can explain why hedge funds and other investors do not take large counter-cyclical positions in the aggregate equity market, but they leave open the question of who buys when markets crash and who sells during booms. We address this long-standing puzzle using transaction-level data on the universe of trades across major European equity and interest rate cash and derivatives markets. We classify investors into sectors and quantify, at horizons from 15 minutes to one month, which sectors absorb the net flows of the most pro-cyclical sector (asset managers). We find dealer banks are the dominant liquidity providers across markets. At daily frequency, they absorb nearly the entire net flow of asset managers, while principal trading firms and hedge funds absorb only about 10% and 5%, respectively. Long-term investors trade pro-cyclically, amplifying rather than offsetting flows. Our results provide direct evidence for the conjecture in the intermediary asset pricing literature that dealer banks are key liquidity providers across different asset classes.

Presentations: 14th MSUFCU Conference on Financial Institutions and Investments (2025)
